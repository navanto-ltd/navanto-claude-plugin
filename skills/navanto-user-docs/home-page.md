Signing in puts you on your home page. It answers two questions: what needs you today, and what has moved since you last looked.

## What needs you

Across the top of the page, **Your approvals** lists every approval currently waiting on your decision, soonest due first, with anything past its due date in red. **Next 7 days** shows the site meetings, deliveries and other [calendar](/docs/calendar/) events coming up across your projects; choosing one opens the event itself, on your calendar.

Alongside them, **Your team** lists the people you work with and how their [tasks](/docs/tasks/) are going, and **Your projects** gives you each live project with its status. Every row is a link — open a person to see their tasks, a project to go to it, an approval to make the decision without leaving what you were doing.

Each of these shows the first few and then a link to the full screen, so the home page stays a summary rather than a second copy of the app.

## Recent activity

**Recent activity** is the running record of what people have changed: tasks created and finished, approvals decided, drawings uploaded, prices and dates moved, phases and weeks reworked on the [timeline](/docs/timeline/), suppliers and products maintained. It reads as sentences — who did what, on which project — grouped under Today, Yesterday and then by date.

Where a change was an edit rather than something new, a chevron on the right opens it to show what actually changed, old value to new: a price from £2,600.00 to £3,150.00, a due date moved, a task handed from one person to another, a product moved to a different supplier.

When one person works through several of the same kind of thing in a row — adding a batch of tasks, editing a few of them, deleting one — the feed shows it as a single line, *Sam made 6 changes to 3 tasks*. Its chevron lists each task with what happened to it and, for the edited ones, what changed.

Edits that only reshuffle things — dragging phases into a new order, reordering a template's tasks — are left out, so the feed stays a record of what changed rather than a log of every save.

Clicking a row takes you to the thing itself. **View all activity** opens the full history, which keeps loading further back as you scroll.

> Activity covers the projects you are on, plus the organisation-wide things that sit outside any one project — your suppliers, products and templates. If a project's activity is missing, you are probably not on its team; ask an administrator to add you.

Deleted items stay in the record so you can see they were deleted, but they are no longer clickable — there is nothing left to open. Deleting a project removes its activity along with everything else it held.
