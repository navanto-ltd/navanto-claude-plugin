An approval puts a document or decision through a fixed chain of people, one after another. You build it as a draft, send it, and each approver in turn approves or rejects it.

## Building one

A draft approval holds a title, a description, a due date, the project it belongs to, file attachments, and up to five approvers added in order with **Add approver**. Nothing is saved while you build; the approval only exists once you send it.

The order matters: approvers see the request one at a time, in the order you list them.

## Sending it

**Send for approval** saves the approval and puts it in front of the first approver.

> Once sent, the approval can't be edited. The title, description, due date, attachments and approver list are all locked, for administrators too. If something is wrong after sending, raise a new approval.

## How it moves

Only the approver whose stage is active sees **Approve** and **Reject**, and they can add a comment with either. Approving passes the request to the next person; when the last approver approves, it is complete.

The first rejection stops the chain, and any later stages are skipped without being asked.

## The approvals waiting for you

A project's Approvals tab shows that project's open approvals, with decided ones hidden until you tick **Show completed**. The Approvals page in the sidebar is your own list instead: every stage currently waiting on you across your active projects, with **Approve** and **Reject** available right on the row.

Deciding from the row does not take a comment; open the approval if you want to leave one.

## Approvals and drawings

Attachments added to an approval also appear on the [Files](/docs/files/) page, named with the approval they belong to and carrying an action to open it.

To put a drawing through a sign-off chain, link its [revision](/docs/drawings/) to the approval. When the approval concludes, a linked revision with a file and no decision of its own yet is marked **Approved** or **Rejected** to match; if several approvals are linked to the same revision, they must all agree before it moves.
