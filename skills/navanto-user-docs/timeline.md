The Timeline is the project's programme as a week-by-week chart. You build it from **phases** — first fix, say — each drawn in its own colour, and each phase can have several **tracks** when work runs in parallel, one row per floor or plot for instance. The chart runs on weeks: click a week on a track to note what is happening and light it up in the phase's colour.

Anyone whose role includes Timeline can edit it. The project's start and handover dates are drawn on the chart, today is marked whenever it falls on the programme, and the chart simply keeps extending as you scroll — the **Today** button brings you back when you've wandered.

You don't have to build the phase list by hand each time: a timeline [template](/docs/templates/) holds your usual phases in order, and **Apply template** on the **⋮** menu beside **Add phase** adds them to the end of the chart in one go, each picking up the next free colour. You can also apply one when [creating a project](/docs/projects/), so a new project starts with its programme sketched out.

## Weeks

Clicking a week opens that week's panel: a note of what's happening, and chips for **Active**, **Delivery** and **Key event**. Active is what fills the cell with colour; deliveries and key events show as icons, and a note shows as a dot you can read by hovering. The chips save on the spot, and the note saves when you leave the panel. Typing into an empty week, or marking a disruption on it, switches Active on for you, so a note usually lights the week without a second click.

## Deliveries from orders

A confirmed [order](/docs/orders/) shared with the timeline shows as a delivery in the week it's expected, on the track chosen for it: a truck on the week, the **Delivery** chip shown on, and in the week's panel a line such as "Apex Joinery's delivery of items from Kitchen refit" with the exact day it's expected and the items coming, so everyone on the timeline can see when deliveries are due on site. It doesn't make the week active or change the week's own note. The order is where it's managed — change its expected date and the delivery moves with it — so the chip can't be switched off here; if your role includes Orders, the order number links straight to it. The project's [risk briefing](/docs/projects/#risk-briefings) reads each delivery against the work planned on its track, and can flag an order confirmed for after that work starts.

## Events from the calendar

A [calendar](/docs/calendar/) event put on the timeline shows on its track in every week it spans, as a note, a delivery or a key event. The week's panel lists it under **Events** with when it happens, and **Open event** opens it if your role includes Calendar. A **Delivery** or **Key event** chip set by an event shows on and can't be switched off here; change it on the event. **Active**, **Variation** and **Delay** stay with the week, so an event never lights up a week or marks it disrupted.

## Disruptions

A week can carry one of two disruption marks: **Variation**, for a change in scope, or **Delay**, for slippage — bad weather and the like. A variation shows as a dashed border, a delay as hatching, so a troubled stretch of programme is visible at a glance. Disruptions also feed the project's [risk briefing](/docs/projects/).

## Checkpoints

The **Checkpoints** rail under the chart is for the things that must be verified on site before work moves on — checking dimensions against drawings before the next stage, say. A checkpoint sits before a chosen week with a title and notes; once that week arrives unchecked, it shows **overdue** in red.

**Mark as checked** is the sign-off: confirm it has been checked on site, add a comment if you want, and the checkpoint turns green with who checked it and when. It locks at that point — **Undo** the check if it needs editing again.

## What your client sees

Your client never sees the chart itself. Instead, each phase with active weeks becomes a milestone — its name, colour and dates — shown as a simple timeline on the **My Project** page of their portal, with where the project is now. Everything else — tracks, notes, deliveries, disruptions, checkpoints — stays on your side.

Phases reach beyond the chart too: [site updates](/docs/site-updates/) can **Tag a phase**, tying the diary to the programme. On a [completed project](/docs/projects/) the timeline stays readable but nothing can be changed.
